## 2. For a read, not a decision

These state facts about Florida rather than about Leahy. They still need to be
right, but nothing in them turns on what we offer.

### `/florida/bank-statement-mortgages/`

*What can look different on a Florida file*

- **There is no state return to fall back on** — Florida levies no individual state income tax, so a self-employed borrower has no state return in the file. The federal return is the only tax record available, which is the same document a bank-statement review exists to look past. Where a borrower’s deductions reduce the income shown federally, there is no second filing that tells a different story.
- **Insurance sits inside the qualifying figure** — Windstorm and flood coverage are frequently written as separate policies in Florida, and both belong to the monthly housing figure a file is measured against. The same loan amount can therefore present differently here than in a state where one policy covers everything, which is worth establishing before a documentation path is chosen.

### `/florida/self-employed-mortgages/`

*What can look different for a Florida business owner*

- **The federal filing is usually the only tax record** — Florida imposes no individual income tax, and most businesses taxed as pass-through entities file no state income return either. For a self-employed borrower that often means one tax record exists rather than two — so where deductions reduce the income shown federally, there is no second filing that reads differently.
- **Seasonal swings are normal in parts of the economy** — Hospitality, tourism, construction and the marine trades all run to a season here, and a business in one of them can show months that look uneven next to each other without anything having changed. Where income is reviewed as an average, it is worth saying early which months are the quiet ones and why.

### `/florida/asset-utilization-mortgages/`

*What can change the Florida arithmetic*

- **The tax bill may not be the seller’s tax bill** — When a Florida property changes hands, its assessed value is generally reset toward market value rather than carried over from the previous owner. A listing that shows last year’s taxes can therefore understate what the new owner will pay, and since that figure sits in the monthly housing cost, it changes how much asset coverage a file needs. Worth checking before the numbers are built around it.
- **Holding costs are the other half of the calculation** — Property insurance, any separate windstorm or flood policy, and condominium or association dues all belong to the monthly figure that assets are measured against. Two properties at the same price can ask quite different things of the same portfolio.
- **Income that has already stopped** — Florida has a larger share of retired and semi-retired buyers than most markets, and for them the question is rarely what they earn. Where earned income has ended or is winding down, an asset-based conversation starts from the position they are actually in rather than from a pay history that no longer describes it.

### `/florida/1099-mortgages/`

*A Florida 1099 year is rarely one document*

- **Several payers, not one** — Contractors in construction, hospitality, the marine trades and healthcare often work for more than one payer across a year, which produces several forms rather than a single one. Assembling the set matters more than reading any one of them.
- **Commission arrives after the work** — Real estate is one of the larger contract-income occupations in the state, and a commission is generally paid at closing rather than when the work was done. Months can look uneven for reasons of timing alone, which is worth separating from the question of volume.

### `/florida/dscr-investor-loans/`

*What the rent assumption rests on in Florida*

- **Short-term letting is a local question** — Whether a property may be let on a short-term basis, and on what minimum term, is set locally rather than statewide, and the answer differs between neighbouring municipalities. An income assumption built on nightly letting depends on that answer first.
- **The association may restrict letting as well** — Where the property sits in a condominium or association, its own documents may set a minimum lease term, cap the proportion of units that may be let, or require a waiting period after purchase. That is separate from the local rules and can be the stricter of the two.
- **Peak season is not the year** — Achievable rent can differ substantially between the busy months and the quiet ones. A figure drawn from the strongest part of the year describes that part of the year, and an annual view will usually read differently.

### `/florida/super-jumbo-mortgages/`

*Where Florida changes a high-balance conversation*

- **The threshold is not the same in every county** — Conforming limits are set county by county, and at least one Florida county is designated high-cost with a limit above the national figure. The same loan amount can therefore sit in different territory depending on where the property is, which is worth establishing before the conversation is framed as jumbo at all.
- **Waterfront property is harder to compare** — Coastal and waterfront homes at high values are often genuinely unlike one another, and recent comparable sales can be scarce. Valuation takes longer and carries more discussion than it does for a property in a uniform neighbourhood.

### `/florida/rsu-bonus-commission-income/`

*Equity and bonus income after a move to Florida*

- **The employer is often somewhere else** — Remote and hybrid arrangements mean the employer, the payroll and the equity plan may all sit outside Florida while the property is here. That is a question about where the paperwork comes from, not about whether the income counts.
- **Timing a purchase around a vest** — Where a vest or a bonus is expected within months of a purchase, the order of events affects what can be shown and what can be used. It is a more useful conversation before an offer than after one.
- **Commission concentrated in a season** — In a market where much of the year’s business closes in a few months, commission income can arrive in a shape that an even monthly average does not describe well. The annual figure and the monthly one can tell different stories.

### `/florida/no-ratio-mortgages/`

*What a Florida no-ratio file still turns on*

- **The property review does not change** — Valuation, condition, insurability and any association documentation are reviewed exactly as they would be on any other file. Where the property is a condominium or sits on the coast, that review is often the longer part of the process.
- **Income from several directions at once** — Semi-retired and recently relocated buyers often have income arriving from more than one place and none of it dominant — a partial salary, a distribution, a rental, a pension. The difficulty is rarely that there is too little, but that it does not resolve into one figure.
- **Assets, credit and reserves still apply** — The documentation that remains is still documentation: funds for the deposit and for reserves, credit history, and the source of what is being brought to the table. Those questions are asked in the usual way.

### `/florida/construction-loans/`

*Building in Florida has its own calendar*

- **The building code asks for more** — Florida’s code sets wind-load and opening-protection requirements that are among the most demanding in the country, and they are stricter again in the designated high-velocity zones. That shows up in the specification and therefore in the budget, rather than as an afterthought.
- **Permitting is local and not uniform** — Review times vary between municipalities and counties, and a coastal parcel may need environmental or coastal-construction review on top of the ordinary permit. The schedule a draw timetable is built on should reflect the jurisdiction the property is actually in.

### `/florida/home-loans/`

*The Florida property asks its own questions*

- **The roof is usually the first question** — Insurers in Florida take a close interest in the age and condition of a roof, and an older one can narrow the cover available or change its price considerably. Because the policy has to be in place for the loan to close, a roof question is rarely only a roof question.
- **Flood zone is a designation, not an opinion** — Whether a property sits in a special flood hazard area is set by its mapped designation, and that determines whether flood cover is required rather than optional. Two houses on the same street can fall differently.
- **Inspections the insurer wants, not the lender** — Older properties here are commonly asked for a four-point inspection, and a wind-mitigation report can materially change the premium. Neither is a lender requirement as such, but both sit on the path to a bindable policy and therefore on the path to closing.
- **Work done without a permit** — Enclosed porches, added rooms and replaced systems sometimes turn up without a permit history behind them. That can affect how the property is valued and whether it can be covered, and it is far easier to establish before an offer than during an underwrite.

### `/florida/`

*What makes Florida its own conversation*

- **A market largely made of arrivals** — A sizeable share of purchases here involve someone who did not live in the state a year earlier. That affects what history exists and where it sits — employment, business registration, credit and banking records may all still point somewhere else, even where nothing about the person’s situation has actually changed.
- **Statewide does not mean uniform** — Insurance pricing, association rules, permitting times, flood designations and property-tax outcomes vary sharply between counties and between neighbouring municipalities. Two otherwise similar purchases can present quite differently depending only on where they are, which is why the location question comes early rather than late.
- **The property is often the longer conversation** — In many markets the income question takes the time. Here it is frequently the property: cover has to be available and priced, an association’s documents may need reviewing, and a flood designation or a roof age can reshape the arithmetic. Running both enquiries together tends to work better than running them in sequence.
- **The season affects more than the weather** — When a named storm is approaching the state, insurers commonly stop binding new cover until it has passed. Because a policy has to be in place to close, that can move a completion date for reasons that have nothing to do with the file itself — worth knowing before a date is promised rather than after.

### `/florida/mortgage-payment-amortization-calculator/`

*What this estimate does not include*

- **Insurance is the largest omission** — Property cover, and any separate windstorm or flood policy, is not in the figure above. In parts of the state it is a substantial monthly amount rather than a rounding difference, and it is the item most likely to change an affordability conclusion.
- **Association dues are not in it either** — Condominium or homeowners’ association dues are paid alongside the mortgage and can be significant, particularly where a building has funded reserves or carried out structural work. Any special assessment sits outside the estimate as well.
- **The tax line may not match the listing** — Where a property is changing hands, the assessed value is generally reset toward market rather than carried over, so the previous owner’s tax figure can understate what the next owner pays. An estimate built on the old number will be optimistic.
- **Use it to compare, not to decide** — The estimate is a useful way to see how a change in rate, term or amount moves the payment. It is a weaker guide to whether a particular Florida property is affordable, because the items it leaves out vary so much between properties.

### `/florida/about/`

*Where we are, and how that works from Florida*

- **There is no Florida office** — Leahy Lending operates from one location, in Austin, Texas. The address shown on this site is that office. We do not maintain a Florida branch, and nothing here should be read as suggesting otherwise.

### `/florida/contact/`

*Getting in touch from Florida*

- **The address here is the Austin office** — The address shown on this page is the company headquarters in Austin, Texas. It is the principal address required on mortgage advertising, not a Florida location.
- **Say where the property is** — The single most useful thing to include is the city and state of the property, because licensing, programme availability and the next step all follow from it.
- **Florida runs an hour ahead** — Most of Florida is on Eastern time and the office is on Central, so an early call in Florida is an hour earlier here than it looks. Worth knowing when a lender response is time-sensitive.
- **What a first conversation covers** — Income documentation, the property and how it will be used, and roughly where the numbers sit. Nothing about that first conversation commits anyone to anything.

## 5. Illustrations

Perplexity permits a clearly labelled educational illustration in place of a
case study, and bars anything implying Florida transaction experience. Until a
Florida file closes, this is the sanctioned form.

Three rules, all enforced by the build rather than only intended:

- **No outcome.** Each stops at what a review would look at. One that ends in
  an approval is an implied result, which is the thing the rule prevents.
- **No money.** No amounts, rates or percentages anywhere. A figure reads as
  representative however it is labelled.
- **No person.** The subject is a situation, never a borrower who came to us.

### `/florida/bank-statement-mortgages/`

> A business with a busy season and a quiet one, twelve months of statements, and deposits that include owner transfers between its own accounts.

What a review would consider:

- which credits are business revenue and which are transfers, refunds or one-off items
- how the quiet months sit against the busy ones across a full year
- whether the statement period covers a full cycle of that seasonality
- what the federal return shows, given no state return exists

*Deposits are reviewed and adjusted rather than taken at face value — the total credited to an account is not the figure a review works from.*

### `/florida/self-employed-mortgages/`

> One business, three possible documentation paths: tax returns, bank statements, or a prepared profit and loss statement.

What a review would consider:

- what each path shows about the same twelve months, and why they differ
- how the entity is structured, and which filings therefore exist
- whether the business is registered in Florida or somewhere the owner used to live
- which path the available records can actually support

*The paths are not ranked. Which one fits depends on what the business generates in the way of records, not on which is preferable in the abstract.*

### `/florida/asset-utilization-mortgages/`

> A portfolio held across several accounts, and two Florida properties at a similar asking price — one a single-family house inland, one a coastal condominium.

What a review would consider:

- the association dues on one and not the other
- whether a separate windstorm or flood policy is required
- that the tax figure on each listing may reset on sale
- how recently the assets arrived, and from where

*The portfolio is the same in both cases. What differs is the monthly figure it has to support, which is why the property enters an asset conversation earlier than people expect.*

### `/florida/1099-mortgages/`

> A year made of four payers, one of which stopped mid-year, plus a stretch of employed work over the off-season.

What a review would consider:

- whether the forms together describe continuous work or a gap
- how commission paid at closing relates to when the work happened
- what the employed stretch contributes, and how it is documented
- whether the occupation continued across a move into the state

*A year that reads oddly as either contract or employed income on its own can make sense as both. The assembly is the work.*

### `/florida/dscr-investor-loans/`

> A coastal property with a strong peak season, a quiet off-season, and an association whose documents set a minimum lease term.

What a review would consider:

- what the local rules permit by way of short-term letting
- whether the association is stricter than the municipality
- how an annual view of rent compares with a peak-month view
- whether a lease exists at closing or the figure is market rent

*The rent assumption is settled by the rules before it is settled by the property. A figure drawn from the strongest months describes the strongest months.*

### `/florida/super-jumbo-mortgages/`

> The same loan amount against two Florida properties in different counties, one of them in a designated high-cost county.

What a review would consider:

- the conforming limit that applies in each county
- whether the amount is therefore jumbo in both cases, or only one
- how comparable recent sales are for each property
- how long cover is likely to take to arrange on each

*Describing a loan as jumbo is a statement about where the property is as much as about the amount.*

### `/florida/rsu-bonus-commission-income/`

> A vesting schedule granted by a previous employer in another state, a current employer with no Florida presence, and a purchase planned near the next vest.

What a review would consider:

- which documents sit with the former employer and which with the current one
- what the vesting history shows across the move
- how the timing of the purchase and the vest relate
- whether commission or bonus is concentrated in part of the year

*Where the employer is affects where the paperwork comes from. It is a question about retrieval rather than about whether the income counts.*

### `/florida/no-ratio-mortgages/`

> A file in which the debt-to-income calculation is set aside, and everything else is not.

What a review would consider:

- valuation, condition and insurability of the property
- association documents where the property is a condominium
- assets, reserves and where the funds came from
- credit history and the overall profile

*Setting the ratio aside narrows one calculation. It does not reduce the documentation, and on a Florida property the review of the property itself is usually the longer part.*

### `/florida/construction-loans/`

> A build on a coastal parcel with a draw timetable running from spring through autumn.

What a review would consider:

- what the wind-load and opening-protection requirements add to the specification
- how long the permitting authority for that parcel typically takes
- where the schedule sits relative to storm season
- cover during construction, and the policy that replaces it on completion

*The budget and the calendar are the same conversation here. A specification driven by the code shows up in both.*

## Build controls and their limits

Automated content and build controls help enforce approved copy, structural
requirements, placeholder handling, and selected prohibited phrases. They do not
independently verify substantiation, product availability, legal compliance,
privacy compliance, licensing requirements, testimonial permissions, or
state-specific mortgage-advertising obligations.

A passing build is not a compliance clearance and is not a production approval.
